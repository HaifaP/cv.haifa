Introduction

Palestine is a historic region in the Middle East located east of the Mediterranean Sea and west of the Jordan River. Today, it generally refers to the State of Palestine, which claims sovereignty over the territories of the West Bank (including East Jerusalem) and the Gaza Strip, though its borders remain highly contested and under varying degrees of Israeli control.

Palestine Governorates

Palestine is divided into 16 governorates (muhafazat in Arabic): 11 in the West Bank and 5 in the Gaza Strip. These administrative divisions were established following the 1993 Oslo Accords to organize local governance, public services, and civil administration.

Al-Quds means “the Holy” and is the Arabic name of the city of Jerusalem, the third holiest place on earth for Muslims, because of its association with Muhammad’s Night Journey. Jerusalem was the original direction of prayer for Muslims, before a later revelation changed the qiblah to Makkah.

Al-Khalil (الخليل) is the Arabic name for Hebron, a city in the southern West Bank and one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world. The name "Al-Khalil" means "The Friend," referring to Prophet Ibrahim (Abraham), known as "Khalilullah" (Friend of God) in Islamic tradition.

Yafa (يافا), also known as Jaffa, is an ancient port city on the Mediterranean coast, now part of Tel Aviv-Yafo in present-day Israel. It is one of the oldest port cities in the world, with a history dating back thousands of years, and was historically a major Palestinian Arab city and a center of trade, culture, and citrus export (notably the famous "Jaffa oranges").

Khan Yunis is a city in the southern Gaza Strip, the second-largest city in Gaza after Gaza City. It was named after a caravanserai (khan) built in the 14th century by Amir Yunis, which served travelers and traders along the historic route between Egypt and the Levant.

Rafah (رفح) is a city in the southernmost part of the Gaza Strip, located on the border with Egypt. It's split by the Egypt-Gaza border, with a Palestinian side in Gaza and an Egyptian side in Sinai — families were historically divided by this border, which was drawn during the mid-20th century.

Gaza refers to both a city and the broader Gaza Strip, a narrow coastal territory along the Mediterranean Sea, bordered by Israel to the north and east, Egypt to the south, and the sea to the west. It is one of the most densely populated areas in the world, home to roughly 2 million Palestinians, many of whom are refugees or descendants of refugees displaced during the 1948 Nakba from cities and villages now inside Israel.
